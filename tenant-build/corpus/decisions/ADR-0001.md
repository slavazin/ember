# The layer/tenant boundary is entries versus standing files, both at the repository root

## Context
At genesis the corpus scaffold ships at the repository root, and the seam between the
reusable layer and a tenant's knowledge is the kind of file: standing files carry the
scaffold, entries carry the tenant. No per-tenant directory exists.

## Options considered
### Option A — a `layer/` wrapper directory
Shape: the reusable scaffold under its own node, tenants beside it. Verdict: rejected at
genesis as premature structure.
### Option B — a flat root with a file-kind seam
Shape: scaffold and entries share the root, distinguished only as standing-files versus
entries. Verdict: chosen.

## Decision
The layer/tenant boundary is the distinction between standing files and entries; both
live at the repository root, and no tenant carries a directory of its own.

## Consequences
Keeps the tree flat. Forecloses a visible tenant boundary and gives a seam check no tenant
home to derive tenant vocabulary from.

## Warrant
- False if: the reusability claim needs a visible tenant boundary to demonstrate, or a
  seam check needs a tenant tree to scope its scan (as of PR #1).
